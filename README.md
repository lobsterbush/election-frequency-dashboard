# Election frequency and its political consequences

An interactive dashboard on how often nations hold national elections since 1945,
and a first descriptive look at whether voting more often relates to political
division and political indifference.

Live: https://lobsterbush.github.io/election-frequency-dashboard/

## What it shows

- An interactive scatter of election frequency against turnout and against
  political polarization, with a fit line and correlation that recompute as you
  filter by regime type or search a country.
- A ranking of the most frequent electorates.
- National elections worldwide per year, 1945 to 2024.
- Robustness of the estimates across alternative measures, and a comparison of
  complete-case and multiply-imputed results.

## Data and honesty

Everything shown is real, drawn from the analysis outputs; nothing is invented.
Sources: NELDA 6.0 (Hyde and Marinov, 1945 to 2020), the International IDEA Voter
Turnout Database (extension and turnout, to 2026), V-Dem v14 (outcomes and
covariates), and the World Bank (robustness covariates).

These are descriptive cross-national associations, not causal estimates. Election
frequency is endogenous to regime type, so the patterns here are a starting point
for comparative work, not evidence that holding more elections causes any outcome.
